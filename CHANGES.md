# Changes

## 11/21/2026: version 1

- **One check of the job boards.** 71 public boards, all answered; 5,404 postings, saved as one snapshot with a fingerprint (the page's last line shows its first 12 characters). One company was taken off the list before the check: its owner is a company Nash names in public.
- **Owner check, after that, on the 16 companies with a delivery job title.** None of them, and no owner, parent or sister company I could find, is a company Nash names in public. Two owners could not be fully confirmed from public pages.
- **The second version of the labeling rules,** written after the practice labeling and before any test posting was labeled or any AI call. The practice labeling turned up 12 places where the rules were unclear. Each was settled in `data/LABEL-RULES.md`:
  1. A proof line counts only by its own words; the job title alone does not make a line count. If only the title shows the answer, there is no proof line.
  2. Carrying passengers (rideshare, shuttles, resident transport) is not delivering goods.
  3. Question 1 says "dispatch drivers or technicians"; dispatching other workers, such as security officers, does not count.
  4. Separate carrier companies and owner-operators count as outside the company, even when they work under its name.
  5. Gig drivers working through the company's own app count as its own drivers.
  6. A posting with no delivery word and nothing about deliveries counts as "not said" for question 1.
  7. Moving goods between the company's own sites counts; moving things inside one building, lot, base or campus does not.
  8. Travel to customers for training, consulting or meetings is not a field visit.
  9. "Other work" for question 2 names IT support, program and project management, and general management outside deliveries.
  10. Delivery operations management means delivering goods; "service delivery" is something else.
  11. Mechanics who work on the company's own vehicles count as fleet management.
  12. Customers who rent or ride the company's vehicles are not its drivers.
  The practice set was labeled again under these rules. No test posting had been labeled under the first version. On the practice set, question 1 moved from 22 yes / 7 no / 1 not said to 16 / 13 / 1 (passengers, a security dispatcher, moves inside one base or campus, a scooter repair job).
- **A clearer page design.** Bigger type, fewer words per row, and the test details moved into folds that open on click.
- **The AI step, on 30 practice postings only** (other companies; never shown or scored). A third question (who makes the deliveries) failed my steadiness check twice after rewording, so I cut it. A separate AI review of the practice answers caught one of those failures and a wording slip. The confidence needed to show a quote moved from 50 to 20 percent, because at 50, only 6 of the 15 picked lines would have shown; at 20, 12 of the 15 were good proof. Questions 1 and 2 never changed; putting their answers in reverse order changed 3 of 30 answers each, at my limit of 3 in 30. After the cut, 5 practice postings were read again with the final questions: answers moved by at most 0.03, and the same lines were picked. Questions 1 and 2 were not read again on all 30, to stay inside the call limit I had set (345).
- **A fault the practice review found, fixed.** A brief could show a quoted line as proof that the company runs deliveries even when the AI's answer was no or unsure. It now shows that line only on a sure yes, with a test.
- **Cost of practice:** 280 calls, about $0.042. The rewording rounds used 180 calls against 120 I had planned, which left 60 for the one test run and 5 spare under the limit of 345.
- **Left as they are.** The second labeling of practice postings named eight smaller unclear points (chiefly how generous a list of proof lines should be). The test set was already being labeled, and rules change only on the practice set, so these stayed as written; a disagreement they cause on the test set is logged as "rule unclear", not repaired. Known effect: the lists of good proof lines are generous, which makes the quote score easier to pass; the page says so.
- **The freeze**, before the one test run: the postings, the data rules, the labeling rules, the questions and their cutoffs, the answer key, the second AI's labels, how each disagreement was settled, the sorting code, the scoring code, the title rule and its saved answers, the data code and the reading script were each fingerprinted (`site/data/lock.json`).
- **The one test run.** 60 calls, about $0.0068, no errors. As first scored, counting all 60: the AI matched the answer key on 57, missed none, raised no false alarms and was unsure on 3; the title rule matched 56, missed 1, raised 2 false alarms, unsure on 1. Quotes: 55 good proof, and 1 with no line picked. Groups: 6 Brief ready, 3 Needs a person, 7 Skipped (with the answer key's answers instead: 6 / 2 / 8). Cost in all: 340 calls, about $0.048, against a cap of $1.00.
- **Changes after the freeze.** Each is logged in `site/data/lock.json` with which way it moved each count, and listed on the page in one plain line. No company changed group. One changed the scores:
  - **The scorer now leaves out postings the written rules did not settle**, as my plan for the scoring said it would and the first scorer did not. Where the rules were unclear and the second AI's answer would change a score, that posting leaves that score: 2 from the delivery-work score and 2 from the quote score. Delivery-work score, now on 58: the AI 56 match, 0 missed, 0 false alarms, 2 unsure (was 57 / 0 / 0 / 3 on 60); the title rule 54 / 1 / 2 / 1 (was 56 / 1 / 2 / 1); always the common answer 53 match, 5 false alarms (was 55 and 5). Quotes: 53 good proof, 0 not proof, 1 with no line picked. A note had said every disagreement was settled by the rules; 5 of 6 were not, and the page now says so and lists all 6.
  - The others changed wording or code only: counts of 1 in the singular; plain names; the reasons, the brief and the notes reworded (a Brief ready reason gives the job's first posting date, and the unsure reason names the question); leadership hires listed first and the question for a first message tied to the hire; fuller and plainer notes in the test section; and private file names taken out of the published code.
- **Also changed after the run, outside the frozen files:** Odeko's market label is now wholesale distribution (it supplies coffee shops), not grocery; labels do not affect groups.

## 11/22/2026: version 2

- **Why.** An AI review of version 1, written from a growth lead's point of view, said its briefs leaned on driver jobs and missed the people who buy: Carvana's brief stood on a tow-truck driver while it was hiring logistics managers, and a Director of Home Delivery created 43 days before the check, whose posting says it manages "all outbound carrier relationships", was skipped. Version 2 was written after version 1's results and reviews were seen.
- **What changed in the rules.** Job titles now also include logistics, transportation, routing, route and field technician. A leadership title (manager, director, head of) counts as new for 60 days instead of 30, and leadership jobs are read first when a company has more than 8. Question 1 counts delivery of a company's own goods to its customers or between its own sites through outside freight or parcel carriers it chooses, schedules or manages (labeling rules version 3). An unsure answer sends a company to a person only when the job could still qualify. The plain title rule treats the new title words like "delivery", except that an analyst title counts as not delivery work and a field technician counts as delivery work unless the department is technical.
- **The new companies.** 21 companies with a delivery-type title instead of 16, and 78 postings for the AI instead of 60; 53 of the 78 were also in version 1. None of the 5 new companies, nor any owner I could find, is a company Nash names in public. With version 1's two, three owners could not be fully confirmed (later recounted as five).
- **The answer key.** Written again for all 78 under rules version 3 by two AI helper sessions in clean folders; the blind second labeling by a different AI agreed on 76 of 78 (question 1) and 74 of 78 (question 2); of 6 disagreements the rules settled 2 and were unclear on 4, and the key's answer was kept.
- **Practice.** A new draw of 30 practice postings from other companies (same rule and seed, new title words). The first wording passed every limit. A separate AI review found question 1 broader than the rules the key used, so it was reworded once, before its results were seen, to use the rules' own words and to say carrying passengers is not delivering goods. That fixed the passenger error and brought more false alarms on shipping postings: question 1 then sat exactly at two limits (sure but wrong on 3 of 30; changed on 3 of 30 with the options reversed). Frozen as reworded. Practice cost 185 calls, about $0.023.
